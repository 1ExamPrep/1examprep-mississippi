Mississippi requires a state contractor license from the Mississippi State Board of Contractors (MSBOC) once a job passes a dollar limit: $50,000 for commercial work and new homes, $10,000 for residential remodeling or roofing. You apply to MSBOC first, then pass open-book PSI exams, including the 50-question Law and Business Management exam. Here are the 15 questions Mississippians search most.

## Does Mississippi require a contractor license?

Yes, once a job crosses the state's dollar limits. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors licenses the business that contracts the work. Below the limits, no state license is needed, although cities and counties may still require a local business license and permits. The limits are set by the type of work, as the next answer shows, so the same contractor can need a license for one job and not for another.

## How much work can you do without a contractor license in Mississippi?

The limit depends on the kind of work:

| Work | State license needed above |
|---|---|
| Commercial construction | $50,000, including labor and materials |
| New residential construction | $50,000 |
| Residential remodeling or roofing | $10,000 |
| Fire sprinklers, public job | $5,000 |
| Fire sprinklers, private job | $10,000 |

Do not split one project into smaller contracts to stay under a limit; the board looks at the whole job.

## Who is required to have a contractor's license in Mississippi?

Any business that contracts commercial work over $50,000, builds a new home over $50,000, or remodels or roofs a home for over $10,000. That includes general contractors, specialty subcontractors on commercial jobs above the limit, and home builders. Individual electricians and plumbers are a different case: Mississippi has no statewide journeyman or master license, so they test with the city or county that issues the card.

## Does Mississippi require a general contractor license?

Yes, for commercial work over $50,000. The broadest commercial classification is [Building Construction](/building-construction/), which covers commercial buildings of any size and the concrete, masonry, carpentry, roofing and steel work that goes into them. Contractors who work in more than one state can take the [NASCLA Commercial General Building](/nascla-commercial-general-building/) exam instead, which PSI gives in Mississippi. Either way, the Law and Business exam is still required.

## How do I get a builders license in Mississippi?

Home builders apply for a [Residential Builder](/residential-builder/) license from MSBOC. It covers new homes over $50,000. The PSI Residential Builder exam has 80 questions in 180 minutes, open book, and you also pass the Law and Business Management exam. Remodelers who do not build new homes take the [Residential Remodeler](/residential-remodeler/) license instead, which has a 50-question exam in 150 minutes.

## How do I get my contractors license in Mississippi?

Follow five steps:

1. Pick the classification that fits your work and its dollar limit.
2. Form the business and register it with the Mississippi Secretary of State.
3. Apply to MSBOC. Commercial applicants include financial statements.
4. Wait for PSI to email your scheduling notice after the board approves you.
5. Pass your trade exam and Law and Business at 70 percent, and the board issues the license.

## How much is a contractor license in Mississippi?

MSBOC lists its application and license fees on the current application for your classification, and PSI charges its exam fees when you schedule. The larger costs are usually the business setup, insurance and study materials. Because every MSBOC exam is open book, most candidates buy the approved reference books for their trade, often highlighted and tabbed. Prep packages on this site start at $19.99 for single items.

## How long does it take to get a contractor license in Mississippi?

Most applicants finish in 6 to 10 weeks. Board review comes first, and you cannot book PSI until the scheduling email arrives. While you wait, study: plan on 3 to 4 weeks before the test date. File a complete application on day one, since a missing page stops the review until it arrives.

## How hard is it to get a contractor's license in Mississippi?

The exams are open book with a 70 percent passing score, so the challenge is speed with the books. The Building Construction exam has 80 questions in 180 minutes, a little over 2 minutes each. Candidates who know the trade still fail when they spend 5 minutes hunting through 10 reference books. Tabbed books and timed practice are what make the difference.

## What is the Mississippi Law and Business exam?

The Law and Business Management exam is required of every applicant: 50 questions, 2 hours, 70 percent to pass, open book at PSI. It is built on the Mississippi edition of the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. Reciprocity can waive a trade exam, but it does not waive Law and Business. See the [Law and Business exam prep](/business-and-law/) for the current packages.

## Can I take the exam before I apply?

No. You apply to MSBOC first. PSI emails a scheduling notice only after the board confirms you are eligible, and you cannot book a seat before that email arrives. Book the trade exam and Law and Business on the same day if seats allow.

## How do I become a residential contractor in Mississippi?

Choose between Residential Builder, for new homes over $50,000, and Residential Remodeler, for remodeling over $10,000. Roofers who only roof homes can hold a Residential Roofer license. Each needs the MSBOC application, its PSI trade exam and the Law and Business exam. Residential applicants file a shorter form than commercial applicants.

## Does Mississippi require a business license too?

The state contractor license is separate from local business licenses. Cities and counties issue their own business or privilege licenses and permits, so check with the local office where you work. The contractor license itself is issued to a business, so register the company with the Secretary of State before you apply to MSBOC.

## Do electricians and plumbers need a state license in Mississippi?

No state journeyman or master license exists. Individual electricians and plumbers test with the city or county that issues the card, often on ICC exams. A business that contracts electrical, plumbing or mechanical work above the dollar limits still needs an MSBOC license in that classification, such as Mechanical or Electrical.

## Does Mississippi have contractor license reciprocity?

MSBOC has reciprocal agreements with several state boards that can waive a trade exam for contractors licensed there. Law and Business Management is still required. If you hold a license in another state, ask MSBOC whether an agreement covers your classification before you study for a trade exam you may not need.
